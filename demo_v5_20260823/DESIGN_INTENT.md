# v5 Design Intent — “Choose your pause”

## First-principles job

An unfamiliar visitor does not first need another promise of luxury. They need to answer three quiet questions without working for it: **what is this unusual ASMR / Head Spa experience, which session is right for me, and how do I confidently get there?** The page therefore works as a short sensory selector, not as a generic beauty-spa brochure.

## Brand-recognition system

- **Business-specific signal:** ASMR Massage, Head Spa, A Bit of Both and Scratcher Sessions are the published service vocabulary, anchored by the real public Plean studio image and the practical booking / location facts.
- **Visual language:** ink, aubergine, light peach and amber are derived from the public room’s purple ambient light and warm treatment setting, but used as a restrained editorial system rather than a typical muted spa palette.
- **Audience:** someone deciding whether a sensory appointment feels safe, understandable and worth booking—not someone browsing an abstract “wellness” moodboard.
- **Hero:** exactly one actual public studio image: it establishes that there is a real, local place behind the unfamiliar service.
- **Concept art:** one original nonliteral glass-and-silk image presents “a pause” as a sensory metaphor. It is never described or implied as the studio, practitioner, equipment, outcome or customer.

## Architecture and rhythm

`image-led arrival → choice-led session selector → art-led reassurance → proof-led trust → location-led conversion`.

The selector is intentionally not a row of same-shaped service cards: each choice is a different “starting point” with service information, a clear live-booking action and an oversized typographic cue. The bottom closes with location, days, directions and appointment path—not a generic giant slogan/CTA.

## Explicitly avoided

- Generic serif title over AI spa imagery.
- Repeated card grids, oversized review metric as the entire proof, and “Ready to…” colour-block ending.
- Fake social accounts, unverified pricing, treatment benefits, availability, qualifications, people, facilities or outcomes.
- Decorative motion without a user purpose.

## Closing strategy

`Proof + Reassurance + Conversion`: two dated official-review excerpts, current profile review count observation, address, hours, direct phone/email and a single external Ovatu booking path. Removing the business name still leaves ASMR-specific vocabulary, the actual purple room, the Plean location and the unusual service-selection flow; it is not transferable to a generic salon.
