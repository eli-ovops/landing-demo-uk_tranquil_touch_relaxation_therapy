# Design Intent — Tranquil Touch v3

## The recognisable idea

This is a quiet appointment invitation for the purple-lit treatment room in Plean, not an agency landing page. The real room photograph is the only literal place cue; its reflection, flower arrangement and low light establish an intimate after-hours arrival. Copy uses the business’s unusually specific ASMR Massage, Head Spa and A Bit of Both vocabulary, then hands the visitor to the existing booking service.

## Audience and visual grammar

For a first-time local visitor who is intrigued by the experience but does not yet know its vocabulary. The tone is a tactile appointment note: warm paper, near-black ink, hairline rules, editorial image crop and small service annotations. It is intentionally restrained, with one page-width image field rather than a series of coloured panels.

## Source materials and rights

- Real public studio photograph: official BookApp public hero, used once as the single hero image. `CUSTOMER AUTHORIZATION: NOT VERIFIED`.
- Real public logo: official BookApp public logo, used once in the header. `CUSTOMER AUTHORIZATION: NOT VERIFIED`.
- The generated macro atmosphere image was intentionally excluded from the rendered page after visual review. It remains only as an internal, unused concept asset and is not customer-visible.

## Explicitly avoided

- Huge editorial display headlines as the page’s main device.
- Dark-purple bands, fluorescent CTA buttons, giant review numbers, abstract pill/circle motifs, or three equal service cards.
- Any fake salon, fake therapist, fake testimonial or treatment outcome.

## Rhythm and closing

Arrival image → one-sentence orientation → a vertically typeset service index → verified customer words and map → booking close. The closing is a small appointment card anchored in direct Ovatu routing, rather than a generic full-bleed sales CTA.

**Recognition test:** PASS as a design intent: removing the name still leaves the purple Plean studio, local room address, three uncommon service names, and booking hand-off semantics. It would not describe a generic massage business.
